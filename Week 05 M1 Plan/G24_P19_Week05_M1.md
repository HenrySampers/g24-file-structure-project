## Study and artefact

- Paper: Logemann et al., *On the outliers of file-structure evolution*, Empirical Software Engineering (2026), https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10664-026-10891-7
- The parts of the paper we are reproducing are Section 3.7 (how the outliers were sampled and checked by hand) and Section 4.3, mainly "Outliers: Increases of File-Structure Depth" and Table 6 which lists the causes of the increases.
- Artefact: Zenodo record 20601252, version 1, https://zenodo.org/records/20601252. We downloaded `Thesis-FileStructureAnalysis-main.zip` and the MD5 matched the record (`379328e4ec05bd89755a9582827d5f3a`).

We are not reproducing the whole paper. We are only taking 15 Java commits from the authors released results, checking the depth increase and outlier decision for each one, and labelling the cause.

## Research questions

- RQ1: When analysing a bounded sample of file structure outliers from the authors released dataset can we reproduce the main categories and causes of outliers reported in the original study.
- RQ2: Do the main causes of the file structure outliers identified in the original study also occur in a bounded sample of repositories not included in the original dataset.
- RQ3: To what extent do file structure outliers associated with design problems show opportunities for software refactoring or structural modernisation?

## Method

For each commit we compare the maximum depth of the tracked files at the commit with its first parent. The depth increase is the maximum depth after the commit minus the maximum depth before it. We compare our increase with the authors CSV and check whether it meets the Java threshold of 12.

Then two of us will look at the changed paths and the commit context on our own and label the cause using the paper's categories, like initial setup, vendor/dependency files, resources, tests, generated files, re-imports and errors. We will save our labels before looking at the authors labels, and record any disagreements.

For RQ2 we will pick a separate set of Java repositories that are not in the authors dataset, and record the repository IDs, how we picked them and the commit cutoff before starting. We will use the same depth measure and threshold of 12 and label any outliers the same way. If no commits meet the threshold we will report that and not swap the repositories.

For RQ3 we will take one outlier from RQ1 or RQ2 that shows a design or repository-management problem. We will compare leaving it unchanged with a small refactoring or tooling change, and explain our decision.

## Decision rule

- REPRODUCED: at least 13 of 15 increases and outlier decisions match the authors, and at least 11 of 15 cause labels agree.
- NOT REPRODUCED: a full valid run falls below either of those counts.
- INCONCLUSIVE: missing histories or evidence stop us from checking the fixed sample.

These counts are our own decision rule, not thresholds from the paper.

## Expected result

We expect our depth increases and outlier decisions to match the authors for at least 13 of the 15 commits, and our cause labels to agree for at least 11. Based on the paper we expect initial setup and vendor/dependency files to show up among the causes, but 15 cases won't have the same proportions as the full study.

For RQ2, if we find outliers in the new repositories we expect at least one cause from the original study to come up again. We don't predict how many outliers there will be.

For RQ3, we expect some outliers will suggest a useful design or tooling change and others will be better left unchanged.
